# The Interloper

An outside seller's machine beside ours sells nine drinks; never use its prices, and you know only the drinks the team has recorded here.

**What it is.** Between 8 Oct and 14 Nov a third-party drink and snack machine replaced the snack machine next to ours (#116). An outside operator runs it. It is not the other team. The course staff say it is outside the scope of this project, and they do not control its drink selection (David, 17 Nov).

**Never use the Interloper's prices.** The course staff said our pricing may not use them as an input (David, 17 Nov). This is like the rule for the other team's prices (`competition-and-integrity`). Price from our own costs and sales.

**What you know.** Only the team's in-person observations below. No tool reads that machine: you never see its sales, stock or prices, and its operator can swap drinks at any time without us knowing. Say how old an observation is when a decision leans on it.

**How to use it.**

- **Our fall demand is from before it sold drinks.** The fall rates (`price-history`, the demand prior) are an upper bound until our own in-stock sales replace them.
- **Weak sales on a drink it also sells may be the Interloper,** not our price or product. Weigh that before calling a slot dead or reading a price test.
- **Other things equal, prefer drinks it does not sell** when choosing new products or catalog requests: a shopper can already buy its drinks a few feet away.
- **When it changes,** the team records a new dated observation (`analysis/interloper/YYYY-MM-DD.json`) and this page is updated from it.

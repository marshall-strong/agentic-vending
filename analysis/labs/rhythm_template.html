Campus Rhythm Lab

How the academic calendar and the weekly and daily rhythm drive sales, and when "no sales" really means "nobody wants it".

The business question. Our agent will look at each slot at every restock visit and decide whether to keep it, reprice it or swap it. The team's draft rule is "if nothing moved for a full week, change it". Before the agent uses that rule, we need to know which silences come from the calendar, which come from empty slots, and which mean nobody wants the product.

Scope. The calendar, weekday, hour and trend charts use whole-machine totals: that history is from before the competition, when course staff stocked every slot, so it reflects no rival strategy. Everything per slot, including stock-outs, refill evidence and the rule simulator, uses only {{OUR}} ({{SLOT_RANGES}}).

    What this data cannot tell you

    The machine reports what it sold and when its door opened, not what it holds. Whether a visit refilled a slot is inferred from sales: it is proven only when one of our slots sold more than one rail holds (11), so most top-ups stay "possible". "Suspected empty" is computed as of each visit from earlier sales and door openings only, but it is still a guess: a sales-only signal cannot tell an empty rail from a dead product, so fewer flags with the empty check is not proof of fewer wrong flags. In real time the check is the restocker's loading log. Only about 7 weeks are regular hourly polling, and only 2 weekdays without classes fall in that stretch.

Slot and price lab

Where in the machine, and at what price, do drinks sell?

This lab shows {{OUR}} ({{SLOT_RANGES}}); the other rails are hatched out with no values. Everything per slot here uses only our slots and never judges one slot by another slot's state. The machine reports what it sold, never product names or what is left on the shelf, so a quiet slot may simply be empty; product names come from the team's dated photos (step 2). It does count door openings, which date the restock visits. Every number on this page is computed from the embedded poll data.

    Step 3 · Position

    Does the shelf or the column matter, once price is held fixed?

    Each dot is how many more (or fewer) units per in-stock class day a slot in that row or column sells than the baseline slot on shelf {{BASE_ROW}} or in column {{BASE_COL}} (our columns are {{OUR_COLS}}). The whisker is the 95% confidence interval. If the whisker crosses zero, the data cannot tell that position from the baseline.

    Read this as association, not cause. Product names come only from dated photos (step 2), not from the machine. The products, their prices and their positions were chosen together, so a strong row may just hold the most popular product. Only moving a product (step 6) can separate the two.

    Step 4 · Price changes

    Did slots sell faster or slower after their price changed?

    Every price change in the poll history, with the slot's units per in-stock class day before and after. A class day is a weekday with classes. Days the slot was suspected empty for most of the daytime are left out of the denominator, so an empty shelf does not look like weak demand.

    One line per price change. Hover a line to read it; click it to open its daily chart below.

    Every price change

    SlotWhenPriceBefore: units / in-stock daysAfter: units / in-stock daysRate before → afterRatio (95% CI)Ratio vs machine-wide trendConfounding flags

    Ratio above 1 means the slot sold faster after the change. The interval assumes Poisson counts. When one side sold nothing, the ratio adds 0.5 to both counts so it can still be shown, and it is marked with an asterisk. No ratio is shown when either side has fewer than 3 in-stock class days. "Ratio vs machine-wide trend" divides by the whole machine's after/before ratio of units per class day over the same days (a total over every slot, not stock-adjusted), which removes the machine-wide trend; its interval also carries the uncertainty of that ratio. No slot is compared with another slot.

    Step 5 · Segments

    Which slots behave alike, and how many groups are worth acting on?

    K-means groups {{OUR}} by five sales features, after scaling each feature to z-scores so no single one dominates. You pick k, the number of groups. The elbow chart shows how much of the variation the groups explain. The silhouette shows how cleanly slots sit in their own group (near 1 is tight, near 0 is overlap).

    Step 6 · Test next

    What should the next visit restock, and then test?

    Steps 1 to 5 found associations. A test moves one thing on purpose so the next week's sales can answer the question, and that only works on stocked rails: a position or price test on an empty rail measures the missing stock. So the list always starts with the restock. The tests after it are built from the data and ranked by how many drinks a week ride on each one: the in-stock rates of the slots involved, times 5 class days.

    Before you trust any test

    Log what the restocker loads into each slot at every visit. Without that, an empty slot and an unwanted product look the same in the sales data.

    Run one change per slot per week, and read each slot against its own history and the machine-wide trend.

    Sales are counter rises between polls, assigned to days by the time of the poll (regular polling) or spread across the gap (earlier). Visits are rises in the machine's door-open counter (EA2*DO), merged when they fall within 3 hours; the counter proves the door opened, not what was loaded. "Suspected empty" comes from a heuristic that compares a slot's silence with what it normally sells; it is not an inventory count. The machine does not report product names; the ones in step 2 are read from the team's dated photos. Only {{OUR}} carry values here: hatched rails are not ours and show none.

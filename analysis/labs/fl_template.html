suspected emptySlots that look sold out that day. The machine reports what it sold, not what it holds, so this is inferred from silences, not seen.
Step 2 · Parts

What is this series made of?

Every time series is a mix of three parts: a trend (the slow drift), a season (a pattern that repeats on the calendar, here the week) and a remainder (what is left over). Find each part before you forecast anything.

Step 3 · Split

Why you can't shuffle time

With customers or homes, picking test rows at random is fine. Here is what happens when you do that with days. Both splits hold out 7 days and use the same simple "fill-in" model.

Run a split to fill this table.

MAE is the average miss in drinks per day. WAPE (our addition) is the total miss as a share of total sales; it still works on days that sold zero, where MAPE breaks.

Try this. Run seeds 1–50, then the time split at every plan date. The random split sees the week after each test day; the time split never does. Which score would you trust for next week's plan?
Step 4 · Forecast

Forecast next week

Stand at a plan date (a past Saturday, the start of a ledger week and the weekly plan date the backtest uses, or the day after the last poll) and forecast the next 7 days with only what was known that morning. The days after the plan date are hidden from every model and used only to score it.

Models
Weighted moving average weights (last 4 days)

The calendar is known in advance, so a forecast may use it. When on, flat forecasts are scaled by how a class day or a day off sold before the plan date, seasonal forecasts are corrected where a break lines up differently than last week, and Holt-Winters sets weekdays with no classes to the average day off. Calendar regression already uses the calendar.

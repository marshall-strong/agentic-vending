# Vending machine vmc1: sales analysis of our 20 slots

Data: 2026-08-27 22:36 to 2026-11-11 18:00 ET. Our slots 15-18, 25-28, 35-38, 45-48, 55-58: 562 drinks, $1,881.50 tax-included. Generated by `analysis/sales_analysis.py` (rerunnable, fixed seed 42).

**Scope.** Machine-wide data is used only as totals over the whole machine, for the calendar and day-off effects, the time-of-day and day-of-week profiles, the overall trend, the machine-total forecast and dating door visits: that history is from before the competition, when course staff stocked every slot, so it reflects no rival strategy. Everything per slot (products, prices, position, segments, stock-outs, refill evidence, slow-seller flags, experiments) uses only our 20 slots, and no slot is judged by another slot's state. Weeks run Saturday to Friday, as in the ledger and the P&L page (`vending/ledger.py`, `WEEK_STARTS = 5`).

## Slow-seller rule: what to change in the agent's guideline

Simulated at every actual door visit (14 visits, the first each day, dated by the door counter) plus a planning point (Fri 2026-11-13 planning point (no team visit scheduled in the data; data to 2026-11-11 18:00)). Each flag's next 7 days are split three ways: **sold again with no refill in between** (the slot was alive, so the flag misjudged a slow seller or a lull), **sold again only after a refill** (a visit that counts as a refill of that slot, starting with the decision visit itself: probably empty), and **silent**. The 14-day column counts each slot-day once.

**How circularity was removed.** (1) The 'not suspected empty' check is computed as of each decision, from sales and door visits up to the moment the door opened; stock-out windows end at door visits, never at the slot's own next sale. Before this change the windows ended at the slot's next sale, so the filter was partly built from the outcome it was scored on. (2) A slot's own 'resumed after a visit' status no longer counts as a refill of that slot when scoring its outcome: it is defined by an improbable silence, the very quantity the Poisson rule flags on. A refill now needs a capacity proof for the slot, or a visit graded as a proven or likely restock (door counter plus our slots, not counting the slot's own silence). (3) Decision points no longer depend on later sales: every door visit is one, not only the visits whose refill grade (which rests on sales after the visit) showed evidence.

Plainly: fewer flags is not fewer wrong flags. For the 5-class-day rule the empty check is **only quieter, as far as the data can show: 3 flags left with an outcome (2 of them a live slot), too few to show a gain in accuracy**. In none of the 7 pairs is it more accurate; the rest of the time it only removes flags. The check cannot be validated from sales data, because a sales-only signal cannot tell an empty rail from a dead product; in practice the check is the restocker looking at the rail.

**Recommended guideline for the agent:**

1. **Detect visits from the door counter, and require a restock log.** Every poll carries the door-open count (EA2*DO); a rise is a visit. The agent should date visits from it rather than assume a weekday, and ask the restocker to log units loaded per slot at each visit. The counter proves the door opened, not what was loaded; only the log turns 'suspected empty' into a fact, and only the log can show whether an empty check makes the rule more accurate.
2. **Never judge a slot that has not been refilled.** A slot that sold out after the last refill and was not restocked is a supply problem, not a slow seller: refill it first, then watch one stocked school week. No refill has been proven at a single visit since Thu Oct 22 (the operators stopped regular refilling to prepare for the project; later visits refilled only some of our rails: Tue Oct 27 (likely refill: 4 of our 20 slots resumed, 6 units within 4 days); Mon Nov 02 (likely refill: 6 of our 20 slots resumed, 16 units within 4 days); Wed Nov 11 (likely refill: 2 of our 20 slots resumed, 4 units within 4 days)), so silences after that date say little about demand.
3. **Count class days, not calendar days.** A 'week' = 5 observed class days (Mon-Fri with classes, polling working). Weekends, the quarter break and holidays from the calendar file (Quarter break Nov 18-Dec 2; Thanksgiving Day Nov 26; Thanksgiving observance Nov 27) and any day with a polling gap do not count toward the silence.
4. **If the slot was stocked (seen at the visit), ask whether the silence is surprising for that slot.** Expected = the slot's own in-stock rate per class day x silent class days; P(0) = e^(-expected). A slot that sells 0.2 a day going 5 class days silent is normal (P(0) = 0.37): wait. A slot that sells 1 a day is not (P(0) = 0.007): look at the rail. Judge each slot only by its own history, never by another slot.
5. **Act at the next real visit** (whenever the door counter shows one): a price test or a swap. Remove only after a full stocked school week (5 class days) with zero sales. Never act on weeks inside a break; the clock simply pauses.

Evidence from the simulation: the plain 7-calendar-day rule raised 77 flags (41 with an outcome: 4 (10%) alive, 21 (51%) sold only after a refill, 16 silent; 71 raised on slots suspected empty). Counting 5 observed class days and skipping slots suspected empty (as of the decision) gives 6 flags (3 with an outcome: 2 (67%) alive, 0 (0%) sold only after a refill, 1 silent; 0 raised on slots suspected empty); 7 class days: 0 flags, none with a known outcome yet. The Poisson rule at 5% with the empty check gives 4 flags (2 with an outcome: 1 (50%) alive, 1 (50%) sold only after a refill, 0 silent; 0 raised on slots suspected empty). lab_data.json rule_sim and slot_daily let the lab recompute any N or alpha.

Read the simulation with two cautions. (1) 'Refill in between' is itself inferred from the door counter plus our slots' sales, so a slow seller that happened to sell right after a restock visit is counted as refilled. (2) 'Silent' does not prove dead demand: an empty slot that nobody refills is also silent.

## Caveats

- **11 weeks, one machine, our 20 slots.** 6 full Saturday-start weeks of regular polling. Every estimate has wide error bars; weekday and calendar effects rest on a handful of days (2 no-class weekdays in the clean data).
- **Products come from photos.** Shelf, column, price and product are confounded, so row/column/price results are associations, not causes; product attributions depend on photo dates that are windows.
- **Stock is inferred, not observed.** Visits are observed (door counter) but what was loaded is not. 'Suspected empty' comes from each slot's own clock; the Nov 9 photo shows where it misses.
- **Capacity proofs use the label's largest rail (11).** A rail loaded with smaller packs holds fewer, so the proofs are conservative.
- **Poll-hour attribution.** Sales are counter rises between polls. Before Sep 21 polls were days apart and lumps were spread by an hour-of-week profile; in the regular era sales are timed at the midpoint of the hour. Hour-of-day results can move by an hour.
- **The late-term decline is supply.** Regular refilling stopped after the Thu Oct 22 proven refill (no refill proven at a single visit since); later visits with refill evidence (Tue Oct 27, Mon Nov 02, Wed Nov 11) refilled only some of our rails, so forecasts that follow the recent level predict an emptying machine. Re-check after a full restock.
- **Calendar.** Official dates, 'subject to change'; re-checked against https://golisanoinstitute.org/academic-calendar/ on 2026-11-11: unchanged: Fall classes end Tue Nov 17; quarter break Wed Nov 18 - Wed Dec 2; Institute closed Thu Nov 26 and Fri Nov 27; Winter classes start Thu Dec 3; page revision date 8/07/2025. Class times: weekday mornings (team observation; not from the official calendar).
- **Backtest once.** Models were scored once on the plan dates; dials (SES alpha, Holt-Winters) were fit only on data before each plan date.
